## Voice-over

In nineteen eighty-nine, Pepsi traded soda for seventeen Soviet submarines. You see, Pepsi had beaten Coke into the Soviet Union, but rubles could not be traded on world markets. Starting in nineteen seventy-two, Pepsi took Stolichnaya vodka as payment and sold it in America. It worked. Soviets were soon drinking about a billion servings of Pepsi a year. But after a boycott over the war in Afghanistan, vodka alone couldn't pay for all that Pepsi. Then the Soviets offered something they had plenty of: old warships. In spring nineteen eighty-nine, Pepsi took seventeen submarines, a cruiser, a frigate, and a destroyer. The submarines were priced at about one hundred fifty thousand dollars each. You see, Pepsi never wanted a navy. It was only the middleman. So it sold the whole fleet for scrap. The next year, Pepsi signed a three billion dollar deal with the Soviets. Then the Soviet Union collapsed in nineteen ninety-one, and the deal vanished.

## Left out on purpose

- **"Pepsi had the sixth largest navy in the world."** This is a popular myth. 17 old diesel submarines plus three ships were scrapped almost at once, and the ships were never a working navy. It is not stated as a fact anywhere in the video. Optional: add one extra line "No, that did not make Pepsi a navy" if you want to bust the myth for comment bait.
- **Quotes from Pepsi's chairman** (the "disarming the Soviet Union" joke and the "non-existing entity" line): single-source and attributed to a real person.
- **Who bought the scrap** (sources say Norwegian, one says Swedish) and the oil tankers some sites add.
- **Exact vodka boycott date.** Sources disagree on the timing, so the line stays general.
- **The Coke lockout dates** (1972 vs 1985 in different accounts).

## Wording to watch

- "About a billion servings a year" has two sources, one only partly visible in the snippet. Worth a quick check before upload.
- "Priced at about $150,000 each" is the 1989 New York Times figure as repeated by others.
- Say "decommissioned/old" ships, not "Pepsi's navy".
