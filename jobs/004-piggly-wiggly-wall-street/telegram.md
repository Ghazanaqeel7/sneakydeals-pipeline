NARRATION, SHOT BY SHOT
1. Piggly Wiggly's founder bet ten million against Wall Street.
2. You see, in nineteen sixteen, Clarence Saunders opened a strange grocery store in Memphis.
3. Back then, a clerk fetched every item for you from behind the counter.
4. At Piggly Wiggly, you walked through a turnstile, grabbed a basket, and picked your own food.
5. Every item had a price on it, and you paid at the front.
6. He patented the idea. By nineteen twenty-two, there were more than twelve hundred stores.
7. But then, Wall Street traders bet his stock would fall.
8. So Saunders borrowed ten million dollars and bought up the shares himself.
9. One morning, the price shot from seventy-five dollars to one hundred twenty-four by noon.
10. The traders had to buy shares back, and he owned most of them.
11. Then the New York Stock Exchange stopped trading and gave the traders extra time.
12. The price fell. Saunders lost his company and went bankrupt.
13. But next time you pick your own groceries, remember, that was his idea.

KEY FACTS AND SOURCES
- First Piggly Wiggly, Memphis, 1916; turnstile, baskets, pick your own goods, price on every item, pay at the front. Sources: Wikipedia; Global Financial Data; Chowhound.
- Patented in 1917; over 1,200 stores by 1922. Sources: Wikipedia + Global Financial Data (patent); Global Financial Data + Of Dollars and Data (stores).
- Traders shorted the stock; he borrowed about $10 million and bought 105,000 of about 200,000 shares. Sources: Global Financial Data; Of Dollars and Data.
- Price opened about $75 and hit $124 by noon. Sources: Global Financial Data; Of Dollars and Data.
- NYSE suspended trading and gave short sellers extra days to deliver; he went bankrupt and lost the company. Sources: Global Financial Data; Of Dollars and Data; Wikipedia; Chowhound.
