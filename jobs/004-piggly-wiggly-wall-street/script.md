## Voice-over

Piggly Wiggly's founder bet ten million against Wall Street. You see, in nineteen sixteen, Clarence Saunders opened a strange grocery store in Memphis. Back then, a clerk fetched every item for you from behind the counter. At Piggly Wiggly, you walked through a turnstile, grabbed a basket, and picked your own food. Every item had a price on it, and you paid at the front. He patented the idea. By nineteen twenty-two, there were more than twelve hundred stores. But then, Wall Street traders bet his stock would fall. So Saunders borrowed ten million dollars and bought up the shares himself. One morning, the price shot from seventy-five dollars to one hundred twenty-four by noon. The traders had to buy shares back, and he owned most of them. Then the New York Stock Exchange stopped trading and gave the traders extra time. The price fell. Saunders lost his company and went bankrupt. But next time you pick your own groceries, remember, that was his idea.

## Left out on purpose

- **Exact date of the corner**: Wikipedia says November 1922, the two finance histories say March 1923. We say "one morning".
- **How much he lost** ($9 million vs "$5 million short" vs "$10 million in debt"): sources differ, so we just say "went bankrupt".
- **Jesse Livermore's role**: sources disagree (hired him vs he quit before). Not needed, and keeps a real person out of it.
- **Why it's called "Piggly Wiggly"**: Saunders never explained it; the pig-under-the-fence story is a legend. Left out.
- **The pink mansion sold to the city / the Keedoozle robot store**: fun, but only one source each in our check.
- **"First supermarket ever"**: Piggly Wiggly was the first self-service grocery store; "supermarket" (King Kullen, 1930) is a different claim. The script never says "supermarket".
- **Whether the exchange broke its own rules**: scholars still debate it, so the script only states what it did.
