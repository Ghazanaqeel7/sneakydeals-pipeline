**007 — Wrigley Mailed Free Gum to a Million Strangers**

Hook: "Wrigley mailed free gum to a million strangers."

**Full narration (13 shots, ~49s):**
1. Wrigley mailed free gum to a million strangers.
2. He didn't start in gum — he started in soap, giving away free baking powder with every bar.
3. Customers wanted the baking powder more than the soap.
4. So he dropped soap, and sold baking powder instead.
5. Then he gave away free gum with every order — and the same thing happened again.
6. They wanted the gum more than the baking powder.
7. So he dropped baking powder, and bet everything on gum.
8. In nineteen fifteen, he grabbed every phone book in America.
9. And mailed free gum to every single address inside.
10. One and a half million homes got gum nobody ordered.
11. His logic: if you could afford a phone, you could afford gum.
12. Four years later, he mailed it again — to seven million homes.
13. Today, Wrigley gum is sold in almost every country on Earth.

**Key facts (2 sources each):**
- Wrigley sold soap, then switched to baking powder, then to gum — each time because the free giveaway outsold the paid product. (History.com, CNN Money)
- In 1915 he mailed free gum, unsolicited, to about 1.5 million US homes from phone-book listings. (History.com, PA Center for the Book, CNN Money)
- He repeated it in 1919 to about 7 million homes. (PA Center for the Book, Today I Found Out)

**Left out:** the exact 1919 total varies by source (6M–8.5M) — used 7M, the figure two independent sources agree on. Also left out the "free gum on every child's 2nd birthday" promo — real, but a separate story.
