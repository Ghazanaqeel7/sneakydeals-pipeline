# Demo: The $50 Million Deal Blockbuster Laughed At

**s01** In September two thousand, three men flew to Dallas to sell their company to a giant. The giant said no. Ten years later, the giant was bankrupt.

**s02** This is the story of the fifty million dollar deal Blockbuster laughed at.

**s03** It starts in nineteen eighty-five.

**s04** The first Blockbuster opened in Dallas, Texas. Bright blue and yellow, thousands of tapes on the wall, open late every night.

**s05** By two thousand four, it had about nine thousand stores and roughly eighty-four thousand workers.

**s06** Friday night meant a trip to Blockbuster. Almost every town had one.

**s07** But part of the money came from something customers hated. Late fees.

**s08** In two thousand alone, late fees brought in about eight hundred million dollars. Around sixteen percent of all its revenue.

**s09** Meanwhile, a tiny company was mailing DVDs in red envelopes.

**s10** Netflix was founded in nineteen ninety-seven by Reed Hastings and Marc Randolph. Rent online, get the disc by mail, no late fees.

**s11** But in two thousand, Netflix was losing money. So the founders made a bold pitch.

**s12** They flew to Blockbuster's headquarters in Dallas and met its chief executive, John Antioco.

**s13** The offer: buy Netflix for fifty million dollars. Netflix would run Blockbuster's website, Blockbuster would keep the stores.

**s14** According to Marc Randolph, the Blockbuster team struggled not to laugh. The answer was no.

**s15** So the two founders went home and decided to beat Blockbuster instead.

**s16** Blockbuster fought back. It dropped late fees and launched its own online rental service in two thousand four. For a while, it worked.

**s17** But the stores were expensive, the debt was heavy, and streaming was coming.

**s18** In September twenty ten, carrying about a billion dollars of debt, Blockbuster filed for bankruptcy.

**s19** The next year, Dish Network bought what was left for three hundred twenty million dollars.

**s20** Today, only one Blockbuster is left, in Bend, Oregon. Netflix is worth hundreds of billions.

**s21** The lesson. The money you make from annoying your customers is money a competitor will take away.
