**014 — Nike Paid $35 for Its Swoosh Logo**

Hook: "Nike paid thirty-five dollars for its logo."

Full narration (12 shots, ~45s):
1. Nike paid thirty-five dollars for its logo.
2. Yes, the Swoosh. The one on every shoe.
3. It was nineteen seventy-one. Phil Knight ran a small Oregon shoe company, and he needed a logo.
4. He turned to Carolyn Davidson, a design student at Portland State.
5. She sent him an invoice. Thirty-five dollars.
6. He looked at her design and said, I don't love it, but it will grow on me.
7. He paid. And the Swoosh went onto shoes that same year.
8. The little company became Nike, a global giant.
9. Twelve years later, in nineteen eighty-three, Nike thanked her.
10. A gold Swoosh ring with a diamond. And Nike stock.
11. One sketch, thirty-five dollars at first, then a ring and shares.
12. A great idea can look cheap on day one.

Key facts (2+ sources each):
- She was a Portland State design student; invoice of $35. (ESPN 2016, CNBC 2018, Nike.com)
- Knight: didn't love it, but it would grow on him. (ESPN, CNBC, Nike.com; wording differs slightly, so shown as paraphrase-quote)
- Swoosh first used commercially in 1971. (ESPN, Nike.com, CNBC)
- 1983: gold Swoosh ring (with diamond per ESPN) plus Nike stock. (ESPN, CNBC)

Left out: the $2/hour rate (only Nike.com says it), number of shares and the stock's value (no source gives them), whether she sold shares (ESPN and CNBC disagree).
