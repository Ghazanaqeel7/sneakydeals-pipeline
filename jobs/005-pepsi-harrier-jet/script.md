## Voice-over

Pepsi's ad showed a fighter jet as a prize. You see, in nineteen ninety-six, Pepsi Points could get you T-shirts and leather jackets. At the end of the ad, a teenager lands a Harrier jet at his school. On screen it said: seven million Pepsi Points. A twenty-one-year-old business student near Seattle did the math. The rules let you buy extra points for ten cents each. Seven million points would cost him about seven hundred thousand dollars. A real Harrier was worth more than twenty million dollars. So he got investors to lend him seven hundred thousand dollars. He mailed Pepsi fifteen points and a check for the rest. But Pepsi sent the check back and said the jet was only there to be funny. Then in nineteen ninety-nine, a judge ruled no reasonable person could think the ad was serious. And Pepsi's new ad? The jet now cost seven hundred million points.

## Left out on purpose

- **Who sued first and where** (Florida vs New York, Leonard vs Pepsi first): sources disagree.
- **When he first saw the ad** (late 1995 test vs Feb 1996): sources disagree; we only say the campaign year, 1996.
- **Exact jet value** ($23M / $33M / $37M): sources differ, so "more than twenty million".
- **Todd Hoffman's name and number of investors**: sources differ (one friend vs five investors); "investors" is safe.
- **"Sure beats the bus" line and "Just kidding" text**: not needed; avoids quoting the ad.
- **That the Pentagon would only sell a demilitarised jet**: interesting, but it would need its own shot; left out to keep 54 s.
- **The appeal (2000)**: not needed for the story.
- **How many cans 7M points equals**: sources disagree ($4M vs $11M of soda).

## Wording to watch

- Never say Pepsi "offered" or "promised" the jet: the court ruled it was not an offer. We say the ad "showed" it.
- "The jet was only there to be funny" paraphrases Pepsi's letter ("humorous and entertaining"); not presented as a quote.
- Pepsi only as a plain-text name on signs/cans. No Pepsi logo, no globe look-alike, no real Harrier markings or USMC insignia.
- Generic cartoon student and judge; no likeness of John Leonard or Judge Wood. Not legal advice; the story is history.
