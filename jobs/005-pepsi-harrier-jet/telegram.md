SCRIPT 005 for approval: Pepsi's fighter jet prize

TITLE: Pepsi's Ad Showed a Fighter Jet. He Sent $700,000.
HOOK TEXT ON SCREEN: FIGHTER JET / FOR PEPSI POINTS?!
Length: 13 shots, 153 words, about 54 seconds. Same channel voice.

FROM THE CALENDAR (Fri 13 Nov). I changed the draft title "Pepsi Accidentally Offered a Fighter Jet": Pepsi said the jet was a joke on purpose, and the court ruled it was never a real offer, so "accidentally offered" would be untrue. The new title says only what happened. It is a different story from video 001 (Pepsi submarines): 1990s, a student, a school, a courtroom.

NARRATION, SHOT BY SHOT
1. Pepsi's ad showed a fighter jet as a prize.
2. You see, in nineteen ninety-six, Pepsi Points could get you T-shirts and leather jackets.
3. At the end of the ad, a teenager lands a Harrier jet at his school.
4. On screen it said: seven million Pepsi Points.
5. A twenty-one-year-old business student near Seattle did the math.
6. The rules let you buy extra points for ten cents each.
7. Seven million points would cost him about seven hundred thousand dollars.
8. A real Harrier was worth more than twenty million dollars.
9. So he got investors to lend him seven hundred thousand dollars.
10. He mailed Pepsi fifteen points and a check for the rest.
11. But Pepsi sent the check back and said the jet was only there to be funny.
12. Then in nineteen ninety-nine, a judge ruled no reasonable person could think the ad was serious.
13. And Pepsi's new ad? The jet now cost seven hundred million points.

KEY FACTS AND SOURCES
- 1996 "Pepsi Stuff" ad: teen lands a Harrier at school, caption 7,000,000 Pepsi Points. Sources: Wikipedia (Leonard v. Pepsico); The Hustle; Mental Floss.
- Extra points cost 10 cents each, so 7M points = $700,000. Sources: Wikipedia; Seattle Times (AP, 1999); AOPA Pilot.
- 21-year-old business student from the Seattle area; investors lent him $700,000; he mailed 15 points + a check for $700,008.50 (incl. shipping). Sources: Wikipedia; The Hustle; Mental Floss.
- Pepsi returned the check, calling the jet "humorous"; Judge Kimba Wood ruled for Pepsi on 5 Aug 1999. Sources: Wikipedia; Seattle Times (AP).
- Pepsi changed the ad to 700,000,000 points. Sources: Wikipedia; The Hustle; Mental Floss; AOPA.

LEFT OUT AND WHY
- Who sued first and where (Florida vs New York): sources disagree.
- Exact jet price ($23M, $33M or $37M): sources differ, so "more than twenty million".
- Investor names and how many (one friend vs five): sources differ.
- The student's name (John Leonard) and the judge's name are in the description only; on screen they are generic cartoon people, no likeness.
- Ad quotes ("Sure beats the bus", "Just kidding"): not needed, and we never quote.
