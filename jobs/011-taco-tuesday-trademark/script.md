# 011 — Taco John's Owned "Taco Tuesday" Until Taco Bell Stepped In

hookTitle: ["TACO TUESDAY","WAS OWNED?!"]. Calendar brief: Sun 22 Nov item, pulled forward. NOTE: the calendar said "Taco Bell owned the phrase through a shell company". That is wrong: the owner was Taco John's (Wyoming); Taco Bell was the challenger. Script follows the sourced facts.

## Shot table
| key | spoken text |
|---|---|
| hook | Taco John's legally owned the words Taco Tuesday. |
| s02 | A small chain from Wyoming, with about four hundred restaurants. |
| s03 | It registered the phrase in nineteen eighty-nine, in every state but New Jersey. |
| s04 | Even LeBron James tried to trademark it in twenty nineteen. The answer was no. |
| s05 | The government called the phrase too generic. |
| s06 | But Taco John's already had it, so for decades the taco night belonged to one company. |

| s08 | It asked the government to cancel the trademark, saying nobody should own a common phrase. |
| s09 | Taco Bell said it wanted no money. Only to set the words free. |
| s10 | Taco John's had a choice. Fight a giant, or walk away. |
| s11 | Two months later, on July eighteenth, it gave up the trademark in forty-nine states. |
| s12 | Its boss said, we're lovers, not fighters. The chain also gave forty thousand dollars to charity. |
| s13 | For over thirty years, a phrase on every menu in America had an owner. |

## Left out on purpose
Founder, the 2019 brewery cease-and-desist (single source), the "40-year" claim (Fortune conflicts with 1989), Gregory's NJ story (only needed as "except New Jersey"), exact legal cost (sources differ), Taco Bell's own donation (not reported).

## Wording to watch
Taco John's is not a villain: it registered the phrase legitimately, and it kept using it after abandoning. "Gave up in forty-nine states" is exact. Taco Bell is shown as the challenger, not a hero.
