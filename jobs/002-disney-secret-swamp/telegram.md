SCRIPT 002 for approval: Disney's secret swamp

TITLE: Disney Secretly Bought a Swamp Using Fake Names
HOOK TEXT ON SCREEN: SECRET / SWAMP?!
Length: 12 shots, 158 words, about 56 seconds. Same channel voice.

NARRATION, SHOT BY SHOT
1. Disney bought a secret swamp using fake names.
2. You see, Walt Disney wanted to build a huge new park in Florida.
3. But if sellers heard the name Disney, every acre would cost a fortune.
4. So his team bought land through companies with names like Reedy Creek Ranch Corporation.
5. One company was even called M. T. Lott. Say it fast.
6. Swamp, scrub and cattle pasture went for less than two hundred dollars an acre.
7. Starting in nineteen sixty-four, parcel by parcel, they gathered more than twenty-seven thousand acres.
8. You see, that's about twice the size of Manhattan.
9. Then a reporter, Emily Bavar, asked Walt about the mystery buyer on a press trip in California.
10. In October nineteen sixty-five, her newspaper put it on the front page: the mystery buyer is Disney.
11. So weeks later, Walt, his brother Roy and Florida's governor announced it in Orlando.
12. Walt died in nineteen sixty-six. He never saw that swamp become Walt Disney World.

KEY FACTS AND SOURCES
- Secret buying through fake companies, incl. "Reedy Creek Ranch Corporation" and "M.T. Lott". Sources: AllEars; Lou Mongello's WDW History 101.
- More than 27,000 acres, about twice the size of Manhattan. Sources: The Walt Disney Company (official site); WDW History 101.
- Average under $200 an acre. Sources: The Walt Disney Company ("less than $200"); MousePlanet ("roughly $185").
- Reporter Emily Bavar asked Walt on a 1965 press trip in California; the Orlando Sentinel then put "it's Disney" on the front page in October 1965. Sources: Wikipedia (Emily Bavar); Orlando Sentinel 150 article; MousePlanet.
- Announced 15 Nov 1965 in Orlando by Walt, Roy and Governor Haydon Burns. Sources: Wikipedia (Emily Bavar); MousePlanet.
- Walt died in 1966; Magic Kingdom opened 1 Oct 1971. Sources: AllEars; Wikipedia (Walt Disney); CBS News.

LEFT OUT AND WHY
- "First acre $80, last acre $80,000": only fan sites, sounds like a myth.
- Exact price jumps ($131,000 an acre, "1000%"): each number had only one source, so the script just says "would cost a fortune".
- Walt's reaction to the reporter: accounts differ, and we never invent quotes.
- Reedy Creek district politics: off-story and a current political topic.

LOOK
Swamp with gators and frogs, a land office, a lawyer's office full of fake company folders, a cattle ranch, a big map table, a press trip, a printing press, a hotel press conference, and a dusk ending with a generic castle in the mist. No Disney logos, no Mickey, no real castle copy. Real people appear only as generic cartoon figures.
