**016 — Domino's Promised 30 Minutes. A Jury Ended It.**

Hook: "Domino's promised pizza in thirty minutes or free."

**Full narration (12 shots, ~55s):**
1. Domino's promised pizza in thirty minutes or free.
2. Then a courtroom made them pay seventy-eight million dollars.
3. Domino's was famous for speed. If your pizza was late, it was free. That promise built the brand.
4. Every order had a clock ticking on it, and every driver felt it.
5. In nineteen eighty-nine, a Domino's car and a woman's car collided in St. Louis.
6. She sued. She said the thirty-minute rush made drivers less safe.
7. Domino's said its drivers were never told to speed.
8. But in December of nineteen ninety-three, the jury awarded her over seventy-eight million dollars.
9. Days later, Domino's dropped the guarantee for good.
10. The boss said the promise had become a problem. Customers no longer saw it as safe. So it was replaced with a satisfaction guarantee.
11. The case was later settled for far less, and the amount was never made public.
12. A marketing promise that built the brand was ended by one court verdict.

**Key facts (2+ sources each):**
- 1989 St. Louis crash, woman sued Domino's over the 30-minute rush. (AP 1993, UPI 1994, Snopes)
- Jury awarded over $78 million in Dec 1993. (AP, Southeast Missourian, Deseret News)
- Guarantee dropped days later; replaced by a satisfaction guarantee. (AP, Southeast Missourian, Deseret News)
- Later settled for far less, amount not public. (UPI/Deseret 1994, Snopes)
- Domino's never admitted unsafe driving. (Snopes, AP)

**Left out:** her name, the year the guarantee began (sources disagree: 1979 vs 1984), the unconfirmed $15M settlement figure, the false "child killed" rumour.
Wording: "she said" / "Domino's said", because the crash cause was disputed.
