018 — McDonald's Secretly Collects Rent From Its Own Owners

⚠️ Sources disagree on exact numbers, so I used only rounded figures that all sources support ("about a third", "almost two thirds", "more than nine in ten", "roughly half").

Hook: "McDonald's secretly collects rent from its own franchisees."

Full narration (12 shots, ~45-50s):
1. McDonald's secretly collects rent from its own franchisees.
2. Today, about a third of its revenue comes from rent.
3. It started with an executive named Harry Sonneborn, who worked with Ray Kroc.
4. His idea was simple. Do not only sell franchises. Own the land under them.
5. So McDonald's created a real estate company. It bought or leased land for new restaurants.
6. Then it rented that land to the franchisees.
7. Franchisees pay McDonald's a royalty on their sales. And they pay rent on top.
8. Rent is the bigger part, almost two thirds of what franchisees pay.
9. Today, more than nine in ten McDonald's restaurants are run by franchisees.
10. And McDonald's owns the land under roughly half of all its restaurants.
11. So a franchisee does not just sell burgers. They also pay McDonald's for the ground.
12. Franchisees sell the burgers. McDonald's collects the rent.

Key facts (2+ sources each):
- Sonneborn pushed owning land and renting it to franchisees, via a real estate arm (Wikipedia, Workweek, Footnote Brief).
- Franchisees pay royalty on sales plus rent on top (Workweek, Footnote Brief).
- Rent is about 64% of franchisee payments (Workweek 2021 figures, Footnote Brief 2024 10-K figures).
- About a third of revenue is rent (Wikipedia ~35%, Workweek 35%, Footnote Brief ~39%).
- 93-95% franchised (Workweek, Footnote Brief); land owned under 45-55% of restaurants (same two).

Left out: the famous "we are in the real estate business" quote (no primary source), the 40% markup (one source), the start year (sources disagree), Sonneborn's exact job title (sources disagree), asset dollar figures (they conflict).
Wording: "about", "roughly", "almost" are deliberate.
