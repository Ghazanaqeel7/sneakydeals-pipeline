SCRIPT 003 for approval: The Pudding Guy

TITLE: This Man Bought $3,000 of Pudding for Free Flights
HOOK TEXT ON SCREEN: PUDDING FOR / FREE FLIGHTS?!
Length: 12 shots, 162 words, about 57 seconds. Same channel voice.
From the content calendar (Thu 12 Nov item).

NARRATION, SHOT BY SHOT
1. He bought twelve thousand pudding cups for free flights.
2. You see, in nineteen ninety-nine, David Phillips, a civil engineer in California, spotted a deal.
3. Healthy Choice gave airline miles for its barcodes. Ten barcodes, five hundred miles.
4. Mail them in by the end of May, and the miles doubled.
5. But the cheapest Healthy Choice thing he could find? Pudding cups. About twenty-five cents each.
6. So he drove to discount store after discount store and bought their pudding.
7. Twelve thousand one hundred fifty cups. Just over three thousand dollars.
8. Then came the problem. Every barcode had to be peeled off by hand.
9. So he made a deal with the Salvation Army. Volunteers peeled the labels, and they kept the pudding.
10. Donating the pudding even got him a tax write-off of about eight hundred dollars.
11. The result? More than one point two million miles, and lifetime Gold status with American Airlines.
12. His story even inspired part of a Hollywood movie. All from cheap pudding.

KEY FACTS AND SOURCES
- 1999 Healthy Choice promo: 500 miles per 10 barcodes, doubled in May. Sources: Wikipedia; Snopes.
- Pudding about 25 cents a cup; 12,150 cups for just over $3,000. Sources: Wikipedia; Snopes; Futility Closet; Now I Know.
- Salvation Army volunteers peeled the barcodes and kept the pudding; tax write-off about $800 ($815). Sources: Wikipedia; Futility Closet; Snopes.
- More than 1.2 million miles (1,253,000) and lifetime AAdvantage Gold status. Sources: Wikipedia; Snopes; Futility Closet.
- Inspired a subplot of the 2002 film Punch-Drunk Love. Sources: Wikipedia; Snopes.

LEFT OUT AND WHY
- "Free flights for life": not true; he got miles and lifetime status, so the script says miles.
- "Professor": popular claim, but no source confirms it; we say civil engineer.
- Number of stores (10 or 15) and "Y2K cover story": sources disagree or only one source.
- "He beat the company": the company called the promo a success, so nobody is a villain here.

HOOK NOTE
The playbook says brand name first. Healthy Choice is not famous enough to stop a scroll, so the hook leads with the shock (12,000 pudding cups for free flights) and names the brands in shots 3 and 11. If you want the brand first, reject with a note and I will change it.
