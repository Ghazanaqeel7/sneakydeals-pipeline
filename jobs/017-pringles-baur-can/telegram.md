**017 — The Pringles Can Designer Was Buried in One**

Hook: "The Pringles can designer was buried in one."
Note: the calendar said "inventor", but sources say Baur designed the packaging, so the title says "designer".

**Full narration (12 shots, ~50s):**
1. The Pringles can designer was buried in one.
2. Fred Baur was a chemist at Procter and Gamble in Ohio.
3. He designed the tall tube that Pringles chips come in.
4. And he was proud of it. Very proud.
5. So when he died in two thousand eight, at eighty-nine, he made one last request.
6. Bury me in a Pringles can.
7. His children said yes. But they had a problem. They needed a can, and fast.
8. So hours before the burial, they stopped at a store and bought one. Original flavor.
9. They placed part of his ashes inside the can. The rest went in an urn, buried right beside it.
10. Then the family laid him to rest near Cincinnati, Ohio.
11. A small part of his ashes also went to a grandson, to keep.
12. He spent his career on one can. And he chose to spend forever with one.

**Key facts (2+ sources each):**
- Fred Baur, P&G chemist, designed the Pringles tube. (AP/CBS, NPR, Wikipedia)
- Died May 2008, aged 89; asked to be buried in a Pringles can, proud of his design. (AP/CBS, NPR)
- Children bought an original flavor can hours before the burial. (Snopes, Wikipedia)
- Part of the ashes in the can, rest in an urn buried beside it, near Cincinnati. (AP/CBS, Wikipedia, Snopes)
- Some ashes also went to a grandson. (AP/CBS, Wikipedia, Snopes)
