**015 — IKEA's Flat Pack Started With a Sawed-Off Table**

Hook: "IKEA's famous flat pack started with a sawed-off table."

**Full narration (12 shots, ~50s):**
1. IKEA's famous flat pack started with a sawed-off table.
2. Gillis Lundgren was a designer at IKEA in Sweden.
3. As the story goes, he tried to fit a table in his car. It was too big.
4. So he sawed the legs off.
5. The table fit. And that small moment made IKEA rethink how furniture travels.
6. Shipping big, fully built furniture was a headache. It took up space, and things got damaged.
7. Flat boxes solved both problems.
8. The table went on sale as the Lövet, with its legs in the box.
9. Customers finished the job at home. It caught on.
10. Today IKEA is famous for it.
11. And that little table is still around. IKEA brought it back as the Lövbacken.
12. A table that wouldn't fit in a car helped change how we all buy furniture.

**Key facts (2 sources each):**
- Designer Gillis Lundgren took the legs off a table to fit it in his car. (Dezeen, Inc., Irish Times)
- Sold as the Lövet with legs in the box. (Dezeen, Irish Times)
- It made IKEA rethink delivery; self-assembly caught on. (Inc., IKEA history page)
- Reissued as Lövbacken. (Dezeen, Irish Times)

**Left out:** the year (sources say 1956 and 1959), any claim that IKEA "invented" flat pack (it existed before), the furniture-maker boycott (not confirmed).
Wording: "as the story goes", because IKEA itself calls it folklore.
