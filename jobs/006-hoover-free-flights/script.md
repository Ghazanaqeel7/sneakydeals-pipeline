## Voice-over

Hoover said, buy a vacuum, fly free. You see, in nineteen ninety-two, Hoover's British branch wanted to sell more vacuum cleaners. Spend one hundred pounds, and get two free return flights to Europe. Then it added flights to America. New York or Florida. But the cheapest vacuum that qualified cost about one hundred twenty pounds. The tickets were worth far more. So people rushed in. Hoover expected about fifty thousand claims. Hundreds of thousands came. The tickets cost more than the sales brought in. The bill reached tens of millions of pounds. Then Hoover's American owner, Maytag, stepped in. Three top bosses lost their jobs. Hoover had held about half its market for decades. Its share dropped fast. In nineteen ninety-five, Maytag sold the whole European business to Candy. Two free flights, unbelievable. And the bill was a whole European business.

## Left out on purpose
Exact claimant count and loss figure (sources differ), the Sandy Jack lawsuit and its outcome (no verdict found), the 2004 BBC documentary, sale price, exact market-share fall (50→20% vs "90%"), executives' names, who proposed the idea (sources disagree).

## Wording to watch
"tens of millions" (not one figure); "its share dropped fast" (no percentage); say "the bill was a whole European business" only as the closing flourish: Maytag sold it, it did not collapse. This is history, not investment advice.
