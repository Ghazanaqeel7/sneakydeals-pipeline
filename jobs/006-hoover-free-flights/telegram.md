TITLE: Hoover Gave Away Free Flights. It Cost Them Millions.
HOOK TEXT ON SCREEN: BUY A VACUUM / FLY FREE?!
11 shots, ~144 words, about 52 seconds. Same channel voice. From the content calendar (Sat 14 Nov).
Note: like the Pudding Guy this is a free-flights story, but told from the company's side (it loses). Say if you want a different topic.

NARRATION
1. Hoover said, buy a vacuum, fly free.
2. You see, in nineteen ninety-two, Hoover's British branch wanted to sell more vacuum cleaners.
3. Spend one hundred pounds, and get two free return flights to Europe.
4. Then it added flights to America. New York or Florida.
5. But the cheapest vacuum that qualified cost about one hundred twenty pounds. The tickets were worth far more.
6. So people rushed in. Hoover expected about fifty thousand claims. Hundreds of thousands came.
7. The tickets cost more than the sales brought in. The bill reached tens of millions of pounds.
8. Then Hoover's American owner, Maytag, stepped in. Three top bosses lost their jobs.
9. Hoover had held about half its market for decades. Its share dropped fast.
10. In nineteen ninety-five, Maytag sold the whole European business to Candy.
11. Two free flights, unbelievable. And the bill was a whole European business.

KEY FACTS (2 sources each: Wikipedia, Entrepreneur, Today I Found Out)
- 1992 offer: spend GBP 100, get two return flights, later also US flights. Wikipedia; Entrepreneur; TIFO.
- Cheapest qualifying vacuum about GBP 120; tickets worth far more. TIFO (119.99); Entrepreneur (119, tickets 600+).
- Expected ~50,000 claims, hundreds of thousands came. Wikipedia (300,000); TIFO (about 500,000).
- Cost: tens of millions of pounds. TIFO (about 50M); Entrepreneur (about 70M losses).
- Three top managers lost their jobs. Wikipedia; TIFO.
- Hoover held about half its market, share fell. Wikipedia; TIFO.
- Sold to Candy in 1995. Wikipedia; Entrepreneur (1995 sale).

LEFT OUT (sources disagree or single source): exact claimant count, exact loss figure, the lawsuit outcome, the 2004 TV documentary, sale price, share fall (50->20% vs "90%").
