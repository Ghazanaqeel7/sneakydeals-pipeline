**012 — Costco's $1.50 Hot Dog Hasn't Changed Price Since 1985**

Hook: "Costco has sold the same hot dog for one dollar fifty since nineteen eighty-five."

**Full narration (12 shots, ~45s):**
1. Costco has sold the same hot dog for one dollar fifty since nineteen eighty-five.
2. That's a hot dog and a big soda. The price never moved.
3. Gas went up. Rent went up. Everything else got more expensive.
4. Costco's own CEO has said the hot dog was losing money.
5. So the boss asked if he could raise the price. The answer, as he tells it, was no. Figure it out.
6. He did. Costco built its own hot dog factories.
7. Making them in-house cut the cost, and the price stayed the same.
8. Today it sells hundreds of millions of these combos in a single year.
9. In twenty twenty-three, it was nearly two hundred million.
10. Adjusted for inflation, that hot dog would cost over four dollars today.
11. But you don't come to Costco for a hot dog. You come for the membership and the cart full of stuff.
12. The hot dog was never the product. It was the reason to trust the store.

**Key facts (2 sources each):**
- $1.50 combo since 1985. (NPR, Fortune, Mashed)
- CEO said it was losing money; co-founder said no price rise. (Fortune, NPR — both quote the CEO's own 2018 interview, so I say "as he tells it")
- Own hot dog factories cut the cost. (Fortune, NPR)
- Nearly 200 million combos in 2023. (NPR, Mashed)
- Would cost over $4 with inflation. (NPR ~$4.40, CNN via Mashed ~$4.50)

**Left out:** the exact swearing quote (secondhand), 245M in 2025 (one origin), year of the first factory (sources differ 2008/2009), and any claim that it loses money today (sources disagree).
