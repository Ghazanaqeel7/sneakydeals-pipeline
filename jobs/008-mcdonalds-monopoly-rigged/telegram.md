**008 — McDonald's Monopoly Was Rigged by Its Own Security Chief**

Note: Popeyes and Coca-Cola ideas were skipped (claims could not be confirmed with 2 sources).

Narration:
1. McDonald's Monopoly was rigged by its own security chief.
2. Peel-off game pieces on fries and cups could win a million dollars.
3. Simon Marketing ran the game, and an ex-cop named Jerome Jacobson ran its security.
4. Winning pieces were sealed in envelopes, to stop exactly this.
5. But Jacobson was the man who handled them.
6. In nineteen eighty-nine, he gave a twenty-five thousand dollar piece to his stepbrother.
7. By the mid nineteen nineties, he was selling winners on purpose.
8. He swapped pieces in airport bathroom stalls, then passed them to friends and family.
9. They posed as lucky winners, and paid him a cut.
10. Then the FBI began tapping his phone. They called it Operation Final Answer.
11. In two thousand one, dozens were charged. Over twenty million dollars in prizes were gone.
12. His trial began on September tenth. The next day, the news moved on.
13. The rarest winners in the game were never customers at all.

Key facts (2 sources each): ex-cop security chief at Simon Marketing (Daily Meal, Priceonomics); first theft 1989, $25,000 piece to stepbrother (Priceonomics, All That's Interesting); pieces swapped in airport bathrooms (Priceonomics, ATI); recruits posed as winners and paid a cut (Daily Meal, Priceonomics); Operation Final Answer, dozens charged 2001 (Daily Meal, Priceonomics); over $20 million (Priceonomics, ATI); trial began Sept 10, 2001 (Daily Meal, ATI).

Left out: exact total, sentence length, mob links, arrest count (sources disagree).
